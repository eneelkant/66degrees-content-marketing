# CEO voice (Ben Kessler)

**Mandatory for leadership / external / PR assets only.** Source: `07_CEO_VOICE_BEN_KESSLER.md`. Standalone skill — do not merge into generic style. Company facts still apply.

## When to load

Press releases, media and Forbes answers, executive quotes, website leadership copy, LinkedIn in a leader's voice, analyst or award submissions, client-facing leadership notes, anything Ben Kessler will read or be quoted in.

## Seven rules

1. ROI in the P&L is the headline for outcome claims.
2. Claim the IP: proprietary platforms/programs; full names ("the Paradigm° agentic platform", "FDE University (FDEU)").
3. Leadership and core values first on culture/scaling questions; first person "I" is natural.
4. One global company: credit North America and India together.
5. AI raises people — never replacement framing; human on the loop.
6. Precise, defensible claims ("compression of timelines by 50 to 70 percent" not "50 to 70 percent faster").
7. Warm, complete sentences over clipped punch.

## Additional patterns from Round 2 edits

- Platform is the delivery vehicle; outcome is "production-grade"; model is "platform-led".
- People framed as capacity for clients.
- Teams are "critical", never "behind" / back office.
- FDEs combine business and technical expertise; "first, and most lasting" experience.
- Values as conduct for stakeholders and critical decisions.
- "Google's full-stack AI platform" (Ben's term); "AI-first and AI-native" for client transformation — keep in CEO voice and flag v17 conflict as a warning rather than silently rewriting.

## Checklist before handoff

1. Outcomes land on ROI / P&L?
2. IP named in full and proprietary?
3. Culture/scale answers lead with leadership and values?
4. North America and India both credited?
5. Humans stay in charge, moved to higher-value work?
6. Numbers phrased so they cannot be overread?
7. Warm and complete, not clipped?
8. Delivery credited to the platform, ending production-grade?
9. Internal investment restated as client capacity?
10. Teams described as critical, never back office?
