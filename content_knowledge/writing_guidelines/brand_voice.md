# Brand voice and writing style

**Mandatory** for every asset. Source: `03_WRITING_RULES.md`.

## Reader stance

- ~80% of copy goes to buyers: CxO and VP business decision makers — not hands-on keyboard experts.
- Write as an expert business consultant with engineering depth, from a solution mindset.
- Category leader altitude (McKinsey / Deloitte register). Never sound like a beginner or mid-sized firm.
- Write to **one person**. Campaigns may go wide; the copy must never read as sent to everyone.
- Readers are bottom of funnel. They know their problem. **Never restate it.** Lead with the solution and the outcome they get.
- Winner side always: the reader is already an excellent operator. No fear or pain selling, no punching down, no pressure framing, no theatrics, no manufactured scarcity.

## Merciless reader test

Busy and flooded. Confusion of even 0.1% bins the copy. Every name, team or entity must mean something the instant it appears, or it is cut. Warm, welcoming and optimistic — never needy. Warm is not therapeutic: no filler adjectives, no emotional promises.

## Angle before copy

Find the angle first: the sharp view of the reader's situation that makes this land for the target reader and no one else. Sibling assets on the same theme never reuse the earlier spine.

## Embedded credential (mandatory pattern)

- Never write a standalone statement about 66degrees ("We are a Diamond Partner").
- A 66 fact lives only inside a sentence whose main clause is about the reader, as setting.
- Test: delete the 66 fact. If the sentence still stands and matters to the reader, it is embedded correctly.
- **One credential per asset**, never a stack.
- Same for partners: describe expertise in reader terms, never as a sponsor description.

## Say it once

Each point appears once per asset. If the title lands it, the body proves it with new material. Across a sequence, every piece adds something new. Never bridge with recaps ("Last time we told you").

## Sound human

Avoid AI/mechanical tells: perfectly balanced sentences, tidy triads, summarising abstractions ("every layer of the AI stack"), dressed-up verbs ("convene", "unfolds"), lines that announce their own importance. One thought at a time, plain verbs, concrete nouns, a little loose. Contractionsions are fine. No comma-stacked lists of agenda items.

## People, not brands

Say who the reader will be with ("alongside Google engineers"), not which brand ("with Google"). In our own promotional assets name 66degrees first ("66degrees and Google Cloud"). Every promise is practical and specific to the reader's work. Cut generic consultant prompts ("Tell me what your team is wrestling with").
