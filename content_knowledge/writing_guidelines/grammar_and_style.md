# Grammar and style

**Mandatory.** Source: `03_WRITING_RULES.md`.

## Language

- **No dashes of any kind** (em, en, or hyphen used as punctuation). Exception: "AI-Native" / "AI-native" keeps its hyphen. Product names like Paradigm° keep their symbols.
- US English by default. UK audience assets: British English, UK date format, 12-hour PM times.
- Enterprise register verbs. Never blunt colloquial verbs like "cut"; use reduced, lowered, brought down. No transactional or school-level terms ("bot", "teach").
- Cohesive brevity: one power word replaces a clause.
- No fluff, no jargon, no deep technical detail for buyers.
- **Sentence case** for headings, subjects, titles, CTAs.

## Literal truth

- Every claim must read literally true. Every number traces to the Proof Library or a named source in the brief.
- Never turn an internal aim into a public claim. Never invent social proof ("several guests asked").
- Time words are read when the asset is consumed ("tonight" fails in a video seen before the event).
- Never name internal systems or vendors (e.g. Salesforce) when describing 66 work publicly. State the outcome as a number.

## Structure discipline

State the job of each eyebrow, heading, card and CTA, and where each CTA leads. Each heading makes sense to a reader who lands cold. Clarity beats cleverness. Structure follows the reader's question sequence, ending on the action question.
